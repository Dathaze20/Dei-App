# v0.1.5 notes: find by sight, add in one tap, shelf photos, and the road to real recognition

## 1. What changed on the Counter screen (built)

- **Filter pills replace the collapsible sections.**
  - Under the supplier there is now one row of pills, built from the catalog: `All | 11.5 oz. Slim Can | 12 oz. Can | …` for Arizona, product lines for Frito-Lay/PepsiCo, MEATS/CHEESES for Boar's Head, categories otherwise.
  - The row scrolls sideways when long; the page never does.
  - The chosen pill is remembered per supplier on the phone (`deliordernyc_sys_place.filters`).
  - Search shows every supplier's matches under plain headings.
  - The "Sections" button, its sheet and "Close all" are gone, because the pills do that job.
- **Picture cards (`ProductCard`).**
  - Two per row at normal text sizes.
  - The flavor leads (it tells *Fruit Punch* from *Caution* in the same size), then the product line and the stock line.
  - **+** sits on the picture's corner and adds one case (or item) straight to the order. Once in the order, `[− n +]` moves under the name so it never hides the picture.
  - Where a card is wide enough, it turns into a row with the picture on the left (a CSS container query). This happens at about 130% text on narrower phones and from 160% on the Galaxy A17. At 200% on narrow phones the card stays stacked, with a full-width picture.
- **Compact density stays as the list.** Settings › Appearance › Compact keeps the v0.1.4 rows for anyone who prefers more products per screen. Boar's Head is always the tick list.
- **Favorites removed.** Pins and stars are gone from the supplier bar and the All suppliers grid. Recent suppliers, the remembered supplier and search remain. Saved `favorites` preferences are left untouched (just unused), so nothing is deleted.
- **Stock summary.**
  - The tall yellow "Needs attention: 84 never counted" is now one quiet line: `Stock: 1 low · 84 not counted · Review`.
  - Only verified low or out stock is coloured as urgent; "not counted" means unknown, not low.
  - In Order mode the line appears only when something is verified low or out.
  - Review groups the products as Out, Low, Not counted and Counted a while ago, each with its own **Count these**.
- **Sticky bars at large text.** At 130% text and above, the supplier, mode and pill bars scroll away instead of staying pinned, so products keep the screen.

## 2. Shelf photos (built; honest about what it does)

- **Where:** Menu › Shelf photos, or Count mode › Shelf photos.
- **Photos:**
  - Take photos (camera) or choose several from the gallery, up to 12 per check.
  - Each is resized to 1280 px JPEG (a few hundred KB) and stored in its own IndexedDB database (`deliordernyc-shelf`).
  - Photos are never uploaded and never part of backups.
  - Saved or discarded checks are deleted with their photos after **14 days**. Unfinished checks stay until finished or discarded.
- **Rows:**
  - A row is a product seen in the photos, chosen from a picture list, or proposed from a **barcode read in a photo** (offline ZXing decoder already in the app, loaded only when used).
  - Each row has **Visible in photos** and **Total on shelf (confirm)**. They are separate: six bottles visible is not six bottles total.
  - Rows can be corrected (every change is recorded with its time), or rejected ("Not this product"). Rejected rows are kept for the record and never saved.
- **Saving:**
  - One confirmation writes a verified count only for rows with a product and a confirmed total. The count uses source `photo-review` and goes through the same `saveStockCount` as the keypad.
  - **Undo** in the notice right after takes them back, if they are still the newest counts.
- **No automatic recognition yet,** and the screen says so. Nothing is estimated and shown as counted.
- **Barcode scanning in search:** while searching on Counter, the camera button reads a barcode from a photo and searches its UPC. It matches UPC-A and its EAN-13 form. It only finds products whose UPC is in the catalog; most of the sample catalog has none yet (CSV import can add them).

## 3. Real vision: evaluation and the decision that is needed

**Requirement:** recognise *visible* products on a refrigerator or shelf photo, tell similar flavors apart (Arizona RX *Fruit Punch* vs *Caution*), propose visible counts with uncertainty, and never call an estimate verified.

| Option | Works offline | Flavor accuracy on shelves | Size / speed on a Galaxy A17 | Cost | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Barcode (ZXing, on device)** | yes | exact, but only for barcodes facing the camera (rare on a fridge shelf) | already bundled; ~0.1–0.5 s per photo | free | **built** (shelf photos + search) |
| Generic on-device detector (e.g. COCO-SSD / MobileNet in TensorFlow.js) | yes | counts "bottle"/"can" shapes; **cannot tell flavors or brands** | 5–25 MB model; ~0.5–2 s per photo | free | not built: it cannot do the job; counting generic bottles would invite false confidence |
| Custom on-device model trained on *your* products (TF.js/TFLite, few-shot classifier per product) | yes | good, **if** trained on several real photos of each product in your lighting | 3–10 MB; ~1 s | free to run; needs labelled photos (owner-taken) and a training step | possible next: the Add photos walk already collects the product photos it needs |
| Cloud multimodal vision (e.g. Anthropic Claude or Google Gemini via an owner-controlled backend) | no | good at reading labels and flavors; counts of visible items need checking; does not see hidden rows | network latency ~3–10 s | roughly US$0.001–0.02 per photo depending on the model (≈ US$0.20–4 per 200 photos); confirm current prices before approving | **not built: needs your decision** |

**The cloud option needs, before any code:**
- **Authorization** to create a small backend that holds the API key (a serverless function, e.g. Cloudflare Workers or Google Cloud Run). The key can never be in the APK or on GitHub Pages.
- **A billing account in your name** with the chosen AI provider, and a monthly spending cap.
- **Your choice of what is sent.** Only the shelf photo and the list of candidate product names for the chosen supplier would go; no prices, counts or orders. Each request needs your consent in the app, naming the provider.
- **An evaluation set:** 30–50 real shelf photos with the true visible counts (ground truth), to measure accuracy per flavor before anything is shown as a proposal.

Recommended order:
1. You take product photos with Add photos.
2. We collect an evaluation set from real shelf photos.
3. Either a free on-device classifier is trained on those photos, or, if you approve, the cloud backend above is built.

Both would plug into the same observation rows (`source` = model name, plus a confidence) and the same review and confirm step.

No accuracy number is claimed in this release, because nothing has been measured against real shelf photos yet. The browser tests only prove the review screens and the barcode reader on a generated barcode.

## 4. Live ordering assistant (design, not built)

1. **Flow:** photograph → recognised visible products are proposed (§3) → you confirm counts → the assistant compares verified stock with each product's reorder point and case size → it proposes order lines with the reason ("verified 4, reorder at 6, case of 12") → you accept or reject each → accepted lines go into that supplier's current order. Nothing is ever sent without your final tap on Share.
2. **"What needs checking?"** is answered today by the Stock summary (Review). **"What might need a case?"** uses the same data, plus the signals below.
3. **The floating bubble** keeps its reserved place (docs/V0.1.3_NOTES.md §10). It will not be added until there is something real behind it.

## 5. Honest movement signals (built, shown in a product's details)

- **Frequently reordered:** in 3+ saved orders. Ordering is not selling.
- **Often runs low:** the latest verified count is at or below the reorder point.
- **Movement estimated from verified counts:** "down N in D days". Deliveries in between are unknown, so this is not sales.
- **Bestseller based on actual sales records:** always "not known". The app has no point-of-sale data and never invents it.

A future POS export or feed (CSV of daily sales per UPC) would make the last label real. It would be imported like the CSV catalog, kept on the phone, and labelled as sales.

## 6. Master NYC deli catalog and many stores (design)

**Storage today, measured:**
- The 141 products take 44 KB of JSON (≈313 bytes each).
- 5,000 products would be ≈1.5 MB, about 3 MB in localStorage's UTF-16. That is uncomfortably close to its ~5 MB limit together with orders, history and counts.
- So **at catalog scale the product list moves to IndexedDB** (already used for photos and shelf checks). SQLite (Capacitor plugin) is not needed: IndexedDB handles tens of thousands of records. SQLite would add a native dependency, and the web version could not use it.

**Model** (no code yet; current product ids are kept and become store products):
- `CatalogProduct` (shared, read-only for stores): `catalogId` (stable), brand, line, flavor, size, UPC(s), category, licensed image reference (or none).
- `StoreProduct` (private per store): `id` (today's product id), `catalogId?` (null for the store's own items), `hidden`, supplierId, case size, case cost, retail price, reorder point, owner photo.
- `Supplier` (private per store); `StockCount`, orders, history and shelf checks keep pointing at `StoreProduct.id`, unchanged.
- **Adding from the library** creates a `StoreProduct` linked by `catalogId`. Nothing is assumed in stock and no price is copied unless the library has a licensed list price, which stays labelled as such.
- **Library sources:** only products with known UPC and name, and properly licensed packaging photos. Otherwise owner photos or label tiles. CSV and the Add photos walk are the bulk tools.

## 7. Accounts and subscriptions (design only; nothing shipped)

- **Sign-in:** Google, and Sign in with Apple where offered.
- **Workspaces:** one private workspace per store, with owner and employee roles. A free founder account for you.
- **Billing:** Stripe monthly and annual store subscriptions, checked **on a server**, never only in the app. If the app is ever sold through the Play Store or App Store, their in-app payment rules are checked first.
- **Hosting:** GitHub Pages can keep hosting the static web app, but it cannot hold accounts, payments or a database. That needs a backend, which needs your approval and a budget.
- **Until then:** no login screen, no client-side "subscription" gates.

## 8. Jetro / Grocery Pricer boundary (design only)

- **The future entry point:** one **JETRO** button on the main screen, added only when it works.
- **What DeliOrderNYC gives it:**
  - read-only access to store products (id, UPC, name, size, case size, case cost, supplier);
  - a function to update a product's case cost or retail price, after the person confirms.
- **What Grocery Pricer gives back:** price lookups, supplier costs and margins, as plain records keyed by UPC.
- **No shared storage keys.** Grocery Pricer stays in its own repository and is not touched in this release.

## 9. Data safety in this release

- **Unchanged:** main data keys and the backup format.
- **New:**
  - the `photo-review` count source (older versions read it as text);
  - `deliordernyc_sys_place.filters`, device-only;
  - the separate `deliordernyc-shelf` IndexedDB database, outside backups, with the 14-day retention after a check is closed.
- **Installs over v0.1.4 and v0.1.2** with nothing to migrate. Tests load v0.1.2/v0.1.4 storage and check every key is unchanged.
